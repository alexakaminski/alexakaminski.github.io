Inequality Without Differences: A Network Theory of Global Income Dispersion

Alexa Kaminski

I build a microfounded production network formation model where firms endogenously form and drop supplier links while investing in productivity. The economy converges to a unique stationary distribution for firm productivity, network degrees, and size. In a multi-country extension, the cross-country GDP distribution has a well-defined limit as the number of countries grows. When calibrated, the model produces persistent income dispersion even without cross-country fundamental differences, and it matches observed degree and productivity distributions.

Central Bank Narratives and the Transmission of Monetary Policy

Alexa Kaminski, Alistair Macaulay, Wenting Song

This paper studies how the transmission of monetary policy varies with policymaker narratives. Using an AI-based data classification algorithm, we construct directed graphs of the causal mechanisms described in FOMC transcripts, which capture the narratives used to justify interest rate decisions. We find substantial variation in narratives over time, which has consequences for the power of monetary policy transmission.

Using Media to Estimate Climate Preferences Over Time

Alexa Kaminski, Alistair Macaulay

Public sentiment is difficult to measure: surveys often suffer response issues and bias, measuring sentiment in media data is prone to bias because it often neglects the extensive margin (e.g., more articles are published in crises, biasing the sentiment downward). This paper argues that we can resolve both issues by explicitly using the information revealed by the extensive margin of newspaper data. We apply this methodology to measure public sentiment towards climate change and the green transition.
